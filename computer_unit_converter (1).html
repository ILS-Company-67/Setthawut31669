Computer Units Reference Guide

Comprehensive breakdown of standards, multipliers, and conversion formulas used in computing.

Data Storage (Decimal vs Binary)

Storage manufacturers typically use decimal units ($1 \text{ KB} = 1000 \text{ Bytes}$), whereas operating systems (like Windows) often use binary units ($1 \text{ KiB} = 1024 \text{ Bytes}$).

  • 1 Byte (B) = 8 Bits (b)
  • 1 Kilobyte (KB) = $10^3$ Bytes = 1,000 Bytes
  • 1 Kibibyte (KiB) = $2^{10}$ Bytes = 1,024 Bytes

Network Bandwidth (Bits vs Bytes)

Internet speeds are universally advertised in bits per second (bps), while file download speeds are measured in bytes per second (B/s).

  • To convert Megabits (Mbps) to Megabytes (MB/s), divide by 8.
  • Example: $100 \text{ Mbps} \div 8 = 12.5 \text{ MB/s}$.

CPU Clock Frequency

Clock rate measures the frequency at which a CPU runs cycles, scaling in factors of 1,000.

  • $1 \text{ kHz} = 10^3 \text{ Hz}$
  • $1 \text{ MHz} = 10^6 \text{ Hz}$
  • $1 \text{ GHz} = 10^9 \text{ Hz}$
  • $1 \text{ THz} = 10^{12} \text{ Hz}$

Resolution & Print Density

Converts pixels to physical units based on Dots/Pixels Per Inch (DPI/PPI).

  • $\text{Inches} = \text{Pixels} / \text{DPI}$
  • $\text{Centimeters} = \text{Inches} \times 2.54$
  • $\text{Points} = \text{Inches} \times 72$
